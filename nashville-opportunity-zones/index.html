What is an Opportunity Zone?

An Opportunity Zone (QOZ) is a low-income census tract that a governor nominated and the U.S. Treasury certified for a federal tax incentive. Created by the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, the program lets investors put capital gains — profit from selling stock, real estate, a business, crypto, etc. — into a Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF), which must hold at least 90% of its assets in property or businesses inside these zones.

The 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act made the program permanent ("OZ 2.0"). Each state drew a fresh map from a stricter eligibility list (tract median family income at or below 70% of the area median, or poverty of 20%+ with income under 125%), and the map will now be redrawn every 10 years. Nationwide the new map has 6,576 tracts, down from 8,764 in 2018.

What it does for developers

1 · Defer the tax on a gain

Roll a capital gain into a QOF within 180 days and you owe no tax on it yet. Under OZ 2.0 the deferral is a rolling 5 years from each investment, instead of a fixed date for everyone.

2 · Pay tax on less of it

Hold the fund for 5 years and the deferred gain is cut 10% (30% in a rural fund). Three of the metro tracts here are rural: Sumner 203 and Wilson 304.02 and 307.

3 · Tax-free upside

The big one. Hold the QOF investment 10+ years and the appreciation on the project — the new building, the business — can be sold with no capital-gains tax.

Why it matters for real estate

Ground-up development qualifies directly. To use an existing building, the fund must "substantially improve" it, roughly doubling its basis (excluding land) within 30 months. Rural projects only need to add 50%.

Stacks with other tools

Investors often pair OZ equity with Low-Income Housing Tax Credits, historic tax credits, New Markets Tax Credits, or local PILOT/TIF deals, which helps close the financing gap on harder projects.

Timing on this map

Green tracts get the full 10-year window starting 2027. Blue tracts stay zones the whole time. Orange tracts only qualify for new money through 2028, so a fund has to buy the asset before then.

General information, not tax or legal advice. QOF rules (timing, the 90% asset test, working-capital safe harbors) are detailed; talk to a tax advisor before you rely on them.
